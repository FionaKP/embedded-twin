# Example: Adafruit Feather STM32F405 Express — a real open-source board

This example runs **unmodified, real-world design files**: the Eagle
schematic from Adafruit's
[Adafruit-Feather-STM32F405-Express-PCB](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit-Feather-STM32F405-Express-PCB)
repository, ingested directly (60 components, 59 nets) — no manual netlist
authoring.

What the twin reconstructs from the schematic alone:
- the power chain: USB VBUS → MBR054 Schottky + DMG3405 P-FET mux → VHI →
  AP2112 LDO → +3V3 (and battery → FET path when USB is absent)
- the STM32F405 with its real pin naming (`PC1/ADC123_IN11(5T)` answers to
  `PC1`), D13 LED through its 2.2 kΩ resistor, GD25Q16 SPI flash on SPI1,
  WS2812 NeoPixel, MCP73831 charger, battery divider on PA3

[firmware/selftest.c](firmware/selftest.c) is real register-level C: it
reads the flash JEDEC ID over SPI1, samples the battery voltage via ADC,
and blinks D13 healthy/error codes from WFI sleep.

| scenario | result |
|---|---|
| `usb_selftest` | USB bench power: +3V3 comes up through the diode path, flash answers `C8 40 15`, healthy 200 ms blink, no battery flag |
| `battery_only` | LiPo injected on the JST net via `add_components`: P-FET hands over, ADC detects the pack, 2 h of drain simulated in ~9 s (~1.4 mA avg — WFI duty cycling captured automatically) |

A third scenario (charging) lands once the MCP73831 model is generated by
`twin factory` from its datasheet — the charger is deliberately left
unbound until then.
